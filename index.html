On the craft

Marbling floats colour on a thickened bath called the size. Each colour is mixed with ox gall, a surfactant, so a drop spreads into a disc and pushes every earlier colour aside into veins. Styluses, combs and rakes then draw the floating colour into patterns, and a sheet laid on the surface takes up the whole design in one print. European papers were mordanted with alum first; ebru and suminagashi papers are not.

On the mathematics

The bath here follows the model of Lu, Jaffer and colleagues (Mathematical Marbling, IEEE CG&A, 2012). A drop of radius r at C moves each point P to C + (P−C)√(1 + r²/|P−C|²). A tine moves points along its line by z·ud. Every stroke can be undone exactly, so each pixel is traced back through the whole history of the bath.
